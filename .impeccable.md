## Design Context

### Users

People organizing large, messy family photo and video libraries, including older adults who
may be uncomfortable with technical tools. The core job is to choose where memories live,
understand that processing is private and safe, see clear progress, and find familiar moments
without learning media-management terminology.

### Brand Personality

Warm, trustworthy, effortless. The interface should feel calm and reassuring while producing
premium results. It should reduce anxiety around large libraries and never make users feel that
they can break or lose their originals.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light-first, editorial family-archive aesthetic: warm paper neutrals, deep ink text, restrained
marigold accents, generous type, and photography as the dominant visual material. Avoid dark
technical dashboards, neon AI styling, dense settings, tiny controls, and card-heavy admin UI.
Support dark mode later, but make the first product experience exceptionally polished in light
mode.

### Design Principles

1. One obvious next action per screen, with large targets and plain-language labels.
2. Always explain what is happening, how long it may take, and that originals stay untouched.
3. Prefer recognition over recall: show folders, faces, dates, and visual examples instead of IDs
   or technical metadata.
4. Make recovery ordinary: interrupted work resumes without warnings that sound catastrophic.
5. Preserve user confidence with strong contrast, keyboard access, reduced-motion support, and
   no critical meaning communicated by color alone.
